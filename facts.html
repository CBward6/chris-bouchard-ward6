Ward 6 · Burlington · October 26, 2026

The facts on where Chris stands.

Short, specific, and on the record. Ward 6 has heard enough general promises — these are positions you can hold Chris to.

🏘️

Pro-Local Business

Economic Priority
  • Push for local hiring preferences in city contracts and RFPs — Ward 6 businesses first.
  • Advocate for tax policies that reduce the city's reliance on residential property taxes by growing the commercial base.
  • Support youth employment initiatives in Ward 6 to keep young people engaged locally.
  • Oppose unnecessary red tape and permit delays that slow down small business openings.
  • Work to reduce overhead in city operations so savings can be passed back to residents and local employers.
🛡️

Community Safety

Public Priority
  • Convene police, community services, and provincial partners at the same table — not in separate silos.
  • Advocate directly to Queen's Park for provincial funding of mental health and housing supports that municipalities cannot afford to carry alone.
  • Reform the escorted e-scooter and e-bike program, which has created documented safety and accountability gaps on Ward 6 streets.
  • Push for community-based early intervention programs that reduce downstream policing costs.
  • Oppose approaches that treat visible homelessness as a bylaw problem rather than a housing and health problem.
📉

Affordability

Fiscal Priority
  • Push for spending discipline and line-by-line accountability before every budget vote.
  • Call out wasteful programs publicly — the speed camera signage rollout is the example: the city spent money erecting signs after the fact, only for the province to ban the program entirely.
  • Oppose tax increases that are not tied to measurable service improvements Ward 6 residents will actually see.
  • Burlington's property taxes rose 44.91% over the past eight years — a no-vote that still passes every year did not protect Ward 6 families.
  • Support development that generates its own revenue rather than further burdening residential ratepayers.
📍

Community Investment

Infrastructure Priority
  • Coordinate signal timing along Brant Street, Guelph Line, Walkers Line, and Appleby Line — a fixable problem that costs nothing except political will.
  • Push for snow gate systems on residential plows so driveways stop getting buried after every pass.
  • Improve public transit reliability on Ward 6 routes through direct advocacy at the Regional level.
  • Prioritize maintenance of existing parks, trails, and community spaces before new capital projects.
  • Reinvest in the services Ward 6 residents actually use daily, not projects driven by optics.
🏭

Millcroft's Next Chapter

Ward 6 Issue
  • Demand public updates from the province on the Ministerial Zoning Order (MZO) process — residents deserve to know where this stands.
  • Require clear, public criteria for any future development phase before a decision is made, not after.
  • Brownfield and underutilized sites must be prioritized before any public greenspace is touched — this is a firm position, not a preference.
  • Commit to a specific next step — not a general statement — whichever way the Minister's decision goes.
  • Aggressively fight to restore Millcroft residents' green space and hold all parties accountable to that outcome.
  • The developer had fences up and construction was planned for spring. This is not a future threat — it is the current reality.
